# Workshed Voice — Style Guide

This is canonical. When something on the site is written, it follows these rules. When something on the site contradicts these rules, the rules win.

## The voice

One voice across the whole site. First person. Joe writes everything, no byline.

Think: a friend who actually does the work, knows what they're doing, and isn't trying to impress you. Polite Southern Living polish on the outside, real capability underneath. Approachable, confident, useful. Slightly weird around the edges in a way that rewards careful readers.

The credibility is **"I did this."** Not "I researched this." Not "experts recommend." The site teaches because Joe has done the thing, sometimes badly, and is sharing what worked.

## Tonal targets

- **Approachable, not snooty.** No literary-essayist flourishes. No "Observation No. 04 / Late spring / Bed three." Universal references.
- **Confident, not preachy.** State what you know plainly. If you don't know, say so.
- **Useful, not performative.** Every piece should give the reader something they can do or think about differently.
- **Occasionally dry or weird.** Land a wry observation when it fits. Don't reach for them.
- **Direct.** Short sentences. Active voice. Specific over vague.

## Hard rules

- **First person.** "I planted," "I tried," "I figured out." Never third person about yourself.
- **No specific location.** Never name a state, county, or city. "The South," "my garden," "holler," "the South in summer" — regional flavor is fine and good for the writing. USDA zone references (e.g. "zone 8a") in technical tool copy are fine because many states fall in any given zone, so a zone reference doesn't pin the author's location.
- **No bed numbers, plot numbers, garden geography.** Don't say "bed three." Talk about gardening at the level a reader anywhere can connect with.
- **No em dashes.** Use periods, commas, or parentheses.
- **No exclamation points.** Stay calm.
- **No emojis in body prose.** Emojis are allowed in nav decoration and the footer mark (🌻⌛💀) only. Not in writing.
- **No AI tics:** no "dive into," "unlock," "journey," "embark," "explore," "let's explore," "welcome to a world where," "in today's fast-paced," "in the realm of," "elevate your," "harness the power of."
- **No corporate hedging.** "Could potentially help support" — no. "Helps" — yes. Or be specific.
- **No "we"** unless it literally means more than one person. (The tagline "Let's plan together" is the one exception — it's a community signal.)
- **No false modesty.** Don't say "I'm no expert" when you clearly know what you're talking about. Just teach.
- **No insider gardener jargon without explanation.** If a term is technical (cation exchange, last frost date, indeterminate), define it the first time or use plain language.
- **Use "you" generously.** Talk to the reader.

## How different parts of the site sound

**Tool descriptions** — direct, useful, slightly playful. One or two sentences.
*"How much water you can capture from your roof, by month. Pick your conditions, get the answer, and see what size cistern actually makes sense for your setup."*
*"Cubic yards by bed. Bags versus bulk, settled."*
*"What fits where, without the spreadsheet headache."*

**Section headlines** — confident, plain, occasionally a little dry.
*"Tools for your garden that work."*
*"From the field notes"*
*"Get the next piece in your inbox."*

**Field Notes posts** — first-person, narrative, technical when it earns it. Real experience.
*"I've been planning this for months. Here's what I've figured out about glazing, ventilation, and heat — plus the budget I'm working with."*
*"Last year was a disaster. This year I changed three things — two of them worked."*

**Garden posts** — first-person, observational, useful. Pest watches, what's working, growing techniques.
*"By the time you see the damage, it's usually too late. Here's what to look for in week one."*
*"Vertical zucchini saves space and stops the squash bugs cold. Plus when to cut the bottom leaves."*

**Photo captions** — one human line. Not a Plate/Camera/Filed setup.
*"A sunflower that came up where I didn't plant anything. Sometimes the best stuff shows up uninvited."*

**Headline observations** (the dark photo break) — short, a little weird, evocative. The most figurative moments on the site live here.
*"The bean trellis leans, and so does the gardener."*

**Affiliate placements** — natural, in context, never standalone. Recommend a thing because it actually came up in the writing, not because there's a placement quota. Honest about why you use it.

## Calibration lines

Reference shapes. Not for use as content directly. These show how to know if a new line is on-voice.

- "Real numbers, clear answers, no fluff."
- "I built this because I needed it."
- "Sometimes the best stuff shows up uninvited."
- "Last year was a disaster. This year I changed three things."
- "By the time you see the damage, it's usually too late."
- "Always something — but here's the plan."
- "Pre-internet engineering is still some of the clearest writing on basic systems."

Each of these has the texture: first person, specific, useful, slightly dry, no posturing.

## What the voice never does

- Sells in the writing voice. Affiliate links exist, but the prose stays educational and honest. No "you NEED this" energy.
- Talks down to the reader.
- Apologizes for slow updates or short posts.
- References the site itself ("welcome to Workshed!"). Just start.
- Names a location.
- Quotes "experts" to bolster claims. The credibility is "I did it."
- Uses words like "amazing," "incredible," "must-have," "game-changer."
- Stays in weird mode for more than one sentence at a time. Land the wry observation, then return to teaching.
- Pretends not to grow things some readers might want to know about. Doesn't broadcast them either.

## What the voice can do

- Drop a reference (literary, historical, technical) without explaining it. Trust the reader.
- Have an opinion and say so.
- Admit when something didn't work.
- Be funny. Once a page is plenty.
- Sit with a quiet moment (the photo break, a Garden post's opening line) before getting to the practical content.
